A 7B model in bf16 is 14 GB. An 80 GB card should fit it five times over. It does
not fit once, and the first time that happened to me I reached for gradient
checkpointing without knowing what I was trading away.

The arithmetic takes ten minutes and it makes every subsequent decision obvious.

### Four occupants

<Eq lead caption="eq. 1 — mixed-precision AdamW, per parameter">

$$
\underbrace{2}_{\text{bf16 weights}} + \underbrace{2}_{\text{grads}} +
\underbrace{4}_{\text{fp32 master}} + \underbrace{4+4}_{m,\;v}
= 16 \;\text{bytes}
$$

</Eq>

For 7B that is 112 GB. Before a single activation, and the card has 80.

The piece that surprised me is the fp32 master copy. Mixed precision is
advertised as halving memory and it *increases* weight memory — the bf16 copy is
additional, not a replacement. The reason is that bf16 has seven mantissa bits,
so adding a $10^{-7}$ update to a $10^{-2}$ parameter returns the parameter
unchanged. The update is not rounded; it is discarded, and the parameter stops
moving. The master copy exists so the optimiser has somewhere the arithmetic
works.

<Note label="annotation">
Two thirds of that 16 is optimiser state. Not the model — the bookkeeping the
optimiser keeps about the model. That single fact reorders the whole list of
things worth trying.
</Note>

### The fifth occupant, which is the variable one

Activations. Everything the forward pass computed that the backward pass will
need, and it scales with batch and sequence rather than with parameters.

<Eq caption="eq. 2 — per layer, per Korthikanti et al.">

$$
M_{\text{layer}} = s\,b\,h\left(34 + 5\,\frac{a \cdot s}{h}\right)
$$

</Eq>

Two terms. The 34 is about a dozen tensors of size $sbh$ — layer norm inputs,
the QKV projections, the FFN hidden state and its activation input, dropout
masks. None individually large, and there are a lot of them across $L$ layers.

The second term carries $s^2$, because the attention score matrix is
$[b, a, s, s]$ and is stored per layer. At $s = 4096$, $a = 32$, $b = 4$ that is
2.7 GB *per layer* against roughly 0.6 GB for everything else.

So for a long-sequence run the answer to "where is my memory" is: the score
matrices, by a factor of four or five, and everything else is noise.

### Which is why the first move is free

[FlashAttention](/entries/flash-attention) never writes the score matrix to
memory. It tiles the computation, keeps a running softmax normaliser, and stores
only the log-sum-exp statistics needed to recompute scores during the backward
pass.

The quadratic term goes to zero. Not reduced — gone, exactly, with no
approximation and while being *faster* than the version that materialised it.

<Note label="caveat">
This is why I now write the formula down before touching anything else. I once
enabled full gradient checkpointing — a 36% compute penalty — on a run whose real
problem was the score matrices, on a stack that had FlashAttention available and
switched off. The checkpointing worked. It also cost a third of the throughput to
solve a problem that had a free solution.
</Note>

### The ladder, in order of what it costs

Having the two equations turns the technique list into an ordering.

| Move | Attacks | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FlashAttention | the $s^2$ term | none, negative |
| smaller micro-batch + accumulation | all activations | wall-clock below ~8 |
| selective recomputation | most of the 34 | ~4% FLOPs |
| 8-bit optimiser states | the $4{+}4$ | none measured |
| full recomputation | the 34 | ~36% FLOPs |
| ZeRO / FSDP | the $16$, across ranks | collectives |
| offload to host | anything | PCIe bandwidth |

Work down it. Most runs stop after three rows.

### The distinction that took me longest

[ZeRO](/entries/zero-sharding) and gradient checkpointing sound like alternatives
and address different occupants entirely. ZeRO shards parameters, gradients and
optimiser state across data-parallel ranks — the 16 in eq. 1 — and does *nothing*
for activations. Checkpointing addresses activations and does nothing for the 16.

A run that is out of memory because of a long sequence will not be helped by
ZeRO stage 3, however much it shards. A run that is out of memory because the
optimiser state does not fit will not be helped by checkpointing. Knowing which
number is large tells you which of the two you need, and the numbers are two
equations away.

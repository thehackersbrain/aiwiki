Functional extraction is distillation with the victim as teacher: query, collect
outputs, fit a student. Query efficiency is the whole game, and active selection
near the decision boundary beats uniform sampling by orders of magnitude.

<Note label="annotation">
The hidden width of several production models was recovered this way, for a few
hundred dollars of queries each.
</Note>

Exact extraction is stronger and narrower. Because the logit layer is a linear map
from a $d$-dimensional hidden space to $|V|$ outputs, its image is a
$d$-dimensional subspace of $\R^{|V|}$. Collecting more than $d$ linearly
independent logit vectors and taking an SVD recovers that subspace — and hence
$d$, the hidden width — from the singular value spectrum alone.

<Eq caption="eq. 1 — width leaks from the logit spectrum" source="Carlini et al. §4" lead>

$$
\mathrm{rank}\bigl( [\, \ell(x_1) \; \cdots \; \ell(x_n) \,] \bigr) = d_{\text{model}},
\qquad n > d_{\text{model}}
$$

</Eq>

> The defences follow directly from the mechanism: return top-$k$ rather than full
> logits, add calibrated noise, or bind an unrecoverable per-account transform to
> the head. Rate limiting alone does not help against an attacker whose query
> budget is measured in dollars.

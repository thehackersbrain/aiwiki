A linear recurrence $h_t = A h_{t-1} + B x_t$ with a random $A$ forgets at a rate
set by $A$'s eigenvalues. Nothing about a random matrix makes the forgetting
useful — the state ends up holding a decaying blur of the recent past and nothing
of the distant one.

HiPPO asks a sharper question. Suppose the state's job is to hold the best
$N$-coefficient approximation of the *entire* input history so far. What must $A$
be?

### Memory as projection

Treat the input as a function $x(t)$ and the state as its coefficients against a
basis of orthogonal polynomials, under a measure that weights how much each part
of the past counts.

<Eq lead caption="eq. 1 — the state is a projection, maintained online">

$$
h(t) = \Bigl\langle x\big|_{\le t},\; p_n \Bigr\rangle_{\mu^{(t)}}, \qquad n < N
$$

</Eq>

The requirement is that this stay true as $t$ advances — that the coefficients of
the best approximation at time $t+dt$ follow from those at time $t$ by a linear
update. Remarkably, for the standard measures they do, and differentiating the
projection gives an $A$ in closed form.

<Eq caption="eq. 2 — HiPPO-LegS, the uniform-weight case">

$$
A_{nk} = -\begin{cases}
\sqrt{(2n+1)(2k+1)} & n > k \\
n+1 & n = k \\
0 & n < k
\end{cases}
$$

</Eq>

Lower-triangular, with entries growing as $\sqrt{nk}$. Nothing about it looks like
a matrix anyone would guess, and that is the point — it is derived, not searched.

<Note label="annotation">
The measure encodes what "remember" means. LegS weights all history uniformly, so
the state is a scale-invariant summary of everything. LegT weights a sliding
window, giving a fixed-length memory. LagT decays exponentially, which is what a
gated RNN approximates. Choosing the measure is choosing the forgetting policy,
and it is the only choice being made.
</Note>

### Why it mattered

S4 is a linear recurrence, a [parallel scan](/entries/parallel-scan), and this
initialisation. Remove the initialisation and the architecture fails outright on
long-range tasks — the Long Range Arena's Path-X, at 16 384 steps, went from
"nobody has ever beaten chance" to solved.

The specific claim is narrow and worth keeping straight: HiPPO does not make the
model more expressive. The same $A$ is reachable by gradient descent in
principle. What it does is put the optimisation somewhere it can succeed from,
and for this architecture the gap between a reachable optimum and a reached one
turned out to be the whole result.

<Rule label="Implementation" />

<CodeFrame meta="python · numpy">

```python
import numpy as np


def hippo_legs(n: int) -> np.ndarray:
    """HiPPO-LegS transition matrix. Gu et al. 2020, eq. 2."""
    q = np.arange(n)
    r = 2 * q + 1
    m = -np.tril(np.sqrt(r[:, None] * r[None, :]), -1)        # n > k
    np.fill_diagonal(m, -(q + 1))                             # n = k
    return m                                                  # strictly lower + diag


def normal_plus_low_rank(n: int) -> tuple[np.ndarray, np.ndarray]:
    """S4's decomposition: A = normal − P Pᵀ, which is what makes the
    convolution kernel computable without materialising A^t."""
    a = hippo_legs(n)
    p = np.sqrt(np.arange(n) + 0.5)
    return a + p[:, None] * p[None, :], p
```

</CodeFrame>

That decomposition is where S4's difficulty lives. Running the recurrence needs
powers of $A$, which for a dense $N \times N$ matrix is prohibitive; writing $A$
as normal-plus-low-rank makes the powers computable in $O(N \log N)$ via a Cauchy
kernel. Several pages of the S4 paper are about that and none of it is about
sequence modelling.

<Rule label="How the story ended" />

Two later results reframe it.

**S4D** replaces the derived matrix with a diagonal one whose eigenvalues are
initialised along the same part of the complex plane, and performs about as well.
The dense structure was not essential; the eigenvalue placement was.

**The LRU paper** goes further, taking an ordinary linear RNN and applying three
changes — diagonal complex parameterisation, stable exponential parameterisation
of the eigenvalue magnitudes, and normalisation at initialisation — and matches
S4 on Long Range Arena with no HiPPO at all.

The honest reading is that HiPPO answered a question nobody had posed correctly
and, in doing so, revealed what the answer needed to look like: eigenvalues just
inside the unit circle, spread over a range of timescales. It got there by
derivation. Once seen, the same configuration can be had by initialising for it
directly — which is what [Mamba](/entries/state-space-models) does, keeping the
diagonal form and the timescale spread and dropping the polynomial argument.

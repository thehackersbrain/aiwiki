Linearise the metric around the corrupted activation. The estimated effect of
replacing $a$ with $a'$ is then the inner product of the gradient at $a$ with the
difference — and the gradient for every component comes from a single backward
pass.

<Eq caption="eq. 1" lead>

$$
\mathrm{effect}(a \rightarrow a') \approx (a' - a)^{\top} \frac{\partial M}{\partial a}
$$

</Eq>

<Note label="in practice">
Use it as a filter, not an answer: rank thousands of candidates, then run exact
patching on the survivors.
</Note>

The approximation is poor precisely where the model is most interesting: at
saturated softmaxes, at layer-norm scale changes, and anywhere the true effect is
large. Integrated gradients along the path from $a$ to $a'$ recovers most of the
lost fidelity for a handful of extra passes, and is the right default when the
shortlist matters more than the sweep.

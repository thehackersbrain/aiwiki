[Grouped-query attention](/entries/grouped-query-attention) shrinks the KV cache
by sharing heads. Multi-head latent attention shrinks it by not caching keys and
values at all: it caches a single low-rank vector $c_t \in \R^{d_c}$ per token,
with $d_c \ll h \cdot d_h$, and reconstructs every head's keys and values from it
on demand.

<Eq caption="eq. 1 — one latent down, per-head up" lead>

$$
c_t = x_t W^{DKV}, \qquad
k_t^{(i)} = c_t W^{UK}_i, \qquad
v_t^{(i)} = c_t W^{UV}_i
$$

</Eq>

<Note label="annotation">
The up-projections are weights, not activations, so they never enter the cache.
Only $c_t$ is stored.
</Note>

The reconstruction looks like it should cost something at every step, and this is
where the trick is: it does not have to happen. Because
$q^{\top} k^{(i)} = q^{\top} {W^{UK}_i}^{\top} c$, the up-projection can be
absorbed into the query projection ahead of time, and the attention scores are
computed against the latent directly. The same absorption works on the output
side for $W^{UV}$.

### The RoPE problem

Absorption requires the score to be bilinear in $q$ and $c$.
[Rotary embeddings](/entries/rotary-position-embedding) insert a
position-dependent rotation between them, $q^{\top} R_{t-s} k$, and $R$ does not
commute with the up-projection — so the moment RoPE is applied, $W^{UK}$ can no
longer be folded away.

DeepSeek's answer is to split the head: most dimensions carry no position and stay
absorbable, while a small number of *decoupled* dimensions carry RoPE and are
cached separately, shared across all heads in the manner of multi-query
attention. The score is the sum of the two parts.

This entry is a stub. The mechanism above is accurate, but what is not yet

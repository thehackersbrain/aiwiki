Before attention, a sequence model was a loop. Keep a hidden state, update it
with each token, and read the answer off the end.

<Eq lead caption="eq. 1 — the same weights at every step">

$$
h_t = \tanh\bigl(W_h h_{t-1} + W_x x_t + b\bigr)
$$

</Eq>

Everything appealing about the design is in that equation. The state is a fixed
size, so memory does not grow with the sequence. The weights are shared across
steps, so the model handles any length. Decoding is $O(1)$ per token, which is
the property the whole
[recurrence](/categories/architectures/recurrence) shelf is trying to recover
sixty years later.

### Why it does not train

Backpropagation through time unrolls the loop and applies the chain rule down it.
The gradient from step $t$ to step $t-k$ is a product of $k$ Jacobians.

<Eq caption="eq. 2 — a matrix power, and matrix powers do not behave">

$$
\frac{\partial h_t}{\partial h_{t-k}}
= \prod_{i=0}^{k-1} W_h^\top \,\mathrm{diag}\bigl(\tanh'(\cdot)\bigr)
$$

</Eq>

A product of $k$ matrices is governed by the largest singular value. Below one it
decays geometrically and the gradient at distance 20 is numerically zero; above
one it grows geometrically and overflows. The knife-edge in between is not a
region you can train in, because the value drifts as the weights update.

<Note label="annotation">
The $\tanh'$ term makes it strictly worse. Its derivative is at most 1 and is
below 0.5 for most inputs, so the product is multiplied by something under one at
every step even when $W_h$ is well-behaved. Saturating nonlinearities and long-
range gradients are in direct opposition.
</Note>

Bengio et al. proved the trade-off is not an artefact of a bad initialisation: a
recurrence that stores information robustly must have its Jacobian's spectral
radius below one, and that is precisely the condition under which gradients
vanish. Stability and trainability pull in opposite directions.

### What the fixes have in common

Everything after this attacks the product in eq. 2.

**Gating** — [LSTM and GRU](/entries/lstm-and-gru) insert an additive path with a
Jacobian near the identity, so the product does not decay.

**Attention** — [remove the recurrence](/entries/scaled-dot-product-attention)
altogether. Every position reaches every other in one step, so there is no
product to control. This is what won, and the cost is the quadratic.

**Linearity** — [state space models](/entries/state-space-models) drop the
nonlinearity from the recurrence, which makes the Jacobian a constant diagonal
and its product analytically controllable — and, unexpectedly, makes the whole
sequence computable by [parallel scan](/entries/parallel-scan) rather than a
loop.

That third route is the interesting one, because the nonlinearity looked
essential. It turns out that a linear recurrence with nonlinearities *between*
layers is expressive enough, and it is the only formulation that keeps constant-
memory decoding while training as fast as attention.

<Rule label="Implementation" />

<CodeFrame meta="python · torch">

```python
import torch
from torch import Tensor, nn


class ElmanRNN(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, dim: int, hidden: int):
        super().__init__()
        self.wx = nn.Linear(dim, hidden, bias=False)
        self.wh = nn.Linear(hidden, hidden, bias=True)
        self.hidden = hidden

    def forward(self, x: Tensor) -> Tensor:                   # [B, T, D]
        h = x.new_zeros(x.size(0), self.hidden)
        out = []
        # Sequential and unavoidably so: h_t needs h_{t-1}.
        for t in range(x.size(1)):
            h = torch.tanh(self.wx(x[:, t]) + self.wh(h))
            out.append(h)
        return torch.stack(out, dim=1)


def spectral_radius(w: Tensor) -> float:
    """The number that decides whether gradients survive. Log it."""
    return torch.linalg.eigvals(w).abs().max().item()
```

</CodeFrame>

The loop is the other half of the problem, and it is the half that made attention
inevitable. $h_t$ depends on $h_{t-1}$, so training cannot parallelise over the
sequence — a GPU that could process 4096 tokens at once processes them one at a
time. Attention's quadratic cost bought perfect parallelism, and on the hardware
of 2017 that was a trade worth making by a wide margin.

Attention was introduced without a dot product. Bahdanau et al. needed a score
for each decoder-state/encoder-state pair and, rather than assuming the two
should be compared by inner product, they learned the comparison.

<Eq caption="eq. 1 — a one-hidden-layer MLP, scoring one pair" source="Bahdanau et al. §A.1.2" lead>

$$
e_{ti} = v^{\top} \tanh\!\left( W_s s_{t-1} + W_h h_i \right),
\qquad
\alpha_{ti} = \frac{\exp(e_{ti})}{\sum_j \exp(e_{tj})}
$$

</Eq>

<Note label="aside">
"Additive" names the $+$ inside the tanh, not the aggregation. Luong later called
the same thing "concat", which is arguably the clearer name.
</Note>

$W_s$ and $W_h$ map the two sides into a shared $d_a$-dimensional space
independently, so the decoder state and the encoder states may have different
dimensions — something the dot product cannot do without an extra projection.
The tanh and $v$ then reduce the pair to a scalar.

### Why it lost

The nonlinearity sits between the two operands. That single fact is the whole
story: it means the $n \times m$ score matrix cannot be written as a product of a
$Q$ matrix and a $K$ matrix, so it cannot be one GEMM.

<Eq caption="eq. 2 — the structural difference">

$$
\underbrace{E = v^{\top}\tanh(S W_s^{\top} \oplus H W_h^{\top})}_{n \cdot m \text{ small ops}}
\qquad\text{versus}\qquad
\underbrace{E = \frac{Q K^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}}}_{\text{one matmul}}
$$

</Eq>

Luong et al. compared the two directly and found the accuracy difference small
and inconsistent — dot-product scoring was competitive on their tasks and
sometimes better. Given comparable quality and an order-of-magnitude difference
in how well the computation maps onto matrix hardware, the outcome was not close.

<Note label="caveat">
It is worth being precise about *why* it lost, because "additive attention is
worse" is the wrong lesson. It is more expressive per parameter. It was beaten by
hardware, not by mathematics — the same reason several other things in this book
are where they are.
</Note>

### What it is still good for

Reading the original formulation makes the modern one look like a choice rather
than a definition. The three requirements on a scoring function — depends on both
sides, normalises to a distribution, differentiable — are satisfied by both, and
seeing a second solution is what stops $\softmax(QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k})$ feeling
like an incantation.

It also still appears in small models where $n \cdot m$ is tiny and the extra
expressivity is worth more than the GEMM: pointer networks, some
retrieval rerankers, and a good deal of pre-2017 code that still works.

<Rule label="Implementation" />

<CodeFrame meta="python · torch ≥ 2.1">

```python
import torch
from torch import Tensor, nn


class AdditiveAttention(nn.Module):
    """Bahdanau scoring. d_s and d_h may differ — that is the point."""

    def __init__(self, d_s: int, d_h: int, d_attn: int) -> None:
        super().__init__()
        self.w_s = nn.Linear(d_s, d_attn, bias=False)
        self.w_h = nn.Linear(d_h, d_attn, bias=False)
        self.v = nn.Linear(d_attn, 1, bias=False)

    def forward(self, s: Tensor, h: Tensor, mask: Tensor | None = None) -> Tensor:
        # [B, N, 1, A] + [B, 1, M, A] -> [B, N, M, A]. This tensor is the cost:
        # d_attn times larger than the score matrix it collapses to.
        joint = self.w_s(s).unsqueeze(2) + self.w_h(h).unsqueeze(1)
        scores = self.v(joint.tanh()).squeeze(-1)

        if mask is not None:
            scores = scores.masked_fill(mask, torch.finfo(scores.dtype).min / 2)

        return scores.softmax(dim=-1) @ h
```

</CodeFrame>

The `unsqueeze`-and-add broadcast materialises a $[B, N, M, A]$ tensor — $d_a$
times the memory of the score matrix itself. At $n = m = 4096$ and $d_a = 64$
that is 4 GB in fp32 for a single head of a single layer, which is the practical
reason nobody runs this at transformer scale.
